
## Intro

As of mid July 2026, there were over 65,000 people on immigration detention, but an even larger group of people is on Immigration and Customs Enforcement’s Alternatives to Detention program.

As part of [USA TODAY's immigration tracker](https://data.usatoday.com/projects/dhs-immigration-enforcement-tracker/), we compiled every spreadsheet available on the [Wayback Machine’s](https://web.archive.org/web/20260000000000*/https://www.ice.gov/detain/detention-management) archive of the ICE website going back to 2021.

Here are the trends we found in the use of the alternatives to detention program by ICE's Areas of Responsibilities.

## Findings

- As of mid July, there were over 53,000 people on ankle monitors, as part of the Alternatives to Detention program. That is higher than any other time as far back as our records go. Note that in the latest data release, numbers for the Baltimore AOR are missing.
- The use of ankle monitors was once more common, but the technology was replaced with a phone app, SmartLINK, in recent years. Since 2025, however, ankle monitors started to increase in its share from the total number of people on the program, going from an average of 10% in 2024, to 29% on July 11, 2026, the latest available snapshot.
- The use of the ankle monitors varies widely throughout the country but the top four regions with the highest use are all based around southwest border states: Phoenix, El Paso, Harlingen, and Dallas. In all these, about three-quarter or more of people on the program were on ankle monitors in July
- On the other hand, Seattle, Detroit, Salt Lake City, and Newark were among the regions with the lowest use, between 12% and 14%.
- Nationally, for every person in ICE detention, there’s almost 3 in the alternatives to detention program, as of mid July. In some of the regions by the southwest border, the pattern is flipped, with more people in detention than outside. On the other hand, the New York area has about 34 people in alternatives to detention for every detained person – by far the highest ratio. In all, there’s almost a quarter million people under ICE watch, both from detention and the alternative program.


## Intro

As of early April 2026, there were roughly 60,000 people on immigration detention, but an even larger group of people is on Immigration and Customs Enforcement's Alternatives to Detention program.

As part of [USA TODAY's immigration tracker](https://data.usatoday.com/projects/dhs-immigration-enforcement-tracker/), we compiled every spreadsheet available on the [Wayback Machine’s](https://web.archive.org/web/20260000000000*/https://www.ice.gov/detain/detention-management) archive of the ICE website going back to 2021.

Here are the trends we found in the use of the alternatives to detention program by ICE's Areas of Responsibilities.

## Findings

- As of early April, there were over 46,000 people on ankle monitors, as part of the Alternatives to Detention program. That is higher than any other time as far back as our records go.
- The use of ankle monitors was once more common, but the technology was replaced with a phone app, SmartLINK, in recent years. Since 2025, however, ankle monitors started to increase in its share from the total number of people on the program, going from an average of 10% in 2024, to 26% on April 4, 2026, the latest available snapshot.
- The use of the ankle monitors varies widely throughout the country but the top four regions with the highest use are all based around southwest border states: Harlingen, Phoenix, El Paso, and Dallas. In all these, at least two-thirds of people on the program were on ankle monitors in April.
- On the other hand, Chicago, San Francisco, New York and Seattle were among the regions with the lowest use, between 10% and 20%.
- Nationally, for every person in ICE detention, there's almost 3 in the alternatives to detention program, as of early April. In some of the regions by the southwest border, the pattern is flipped, with more people in detention than outside. On the other hand, the New York area has about 36 people in alternatives to detention for every detained person -- by far the highest ratio. In all, there's almost a quarter million people under ICE watch, both from detention and the alternative program.
